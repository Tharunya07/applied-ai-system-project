# Pet Care Guidelines

## Daily Walk Duration by Pet Size

| Size   | Weight Range    | Recommended Daily Walk Duration         |
|--------|-----------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Small  | Under 20 lbs    | 20–30 minutes (can be split into 2 sessions) |
| Medium | 20–60 lbs       | 30–60 minutes (1–2 sessions recommended) |
| Large  | Over 60 lbs     | 60–90 minutes (2 sessions recommended)  |

**Notes:**
- Walk duration is a minimum; active breeds may need more.
- Puppies should have shorter, more frequent walks (5 minutes per month of age, twice daily).
- Senior pets (7+ years for dogs) should have gentler, shorter walks adjusted to their comfort level.
- Hot weather: avoid walks during peak sun hours (10am–4pm); reduce duration by 25%.

## Feeding Frequency by Species

| Species | Age Group       | Recommended Feeding Frequency            |
|---------|-----------------|------------------------------------------|
| Dog     | Puppy (< 6 mo)  | 3–4 times per day                        |
| Dog     | Adult (6 mo–7 yr)| 2 times per day                         |
| Dog     | Senior (7+ yr)  | 2 times per day (smaller portions)       |
| Cat     | Kitten (< 6 mo) | 3–4 times per day                        |
| Cat     | Adult           | 2 times per day (or free-feeding dry food)|
| Cat     | Senior (10+ yr) | 2–3 times per day (monitor intake)       |
| Rabbit  | Adult           | Unlimited hay (always available); pellets twice daily; fresh vegetables once daily |
| Rabbit  | Young (< 7 mo)  | Unlimited hay and pellets; limit vegetables until gut matures |
| Bird    | Parakeet/Budgie | Fresh food and water daily; seeds/pellets always available |
| Bird    | Parrot (medium) | 2 times per day; fresh fruits and vegetables daily |
| Bird    | Canary/Finch    | Seeds and fresh water daily; greens 3–4 times per week |

**Notes:**
- Always provide fresh, clean water alongside every meal.
- Do not free-feed dogs prone to obesity; use measured portions.
- Rabbit pellets should be limited to 1/4 cup per 5 lbs body weight per day for adults.

## General Grooming Frequency Guidelines

| Activity              | Dogs (Short Coat) | Dogs (Long Coat) | Cats (Short Hair) | Cats (Long Hair) |
|-----------------------|-------------------|------------------|-------------------|------------------|
| Brushing              | Weekly            | Daily            | Weekly            | Daily            |
| Bathing               | Every 4–6 weeks   | Every 4 weeks    | As needed (rare)  | Every 4–6 weeks  |
| Nail trimming         | Every 3–4 weeks   | Every 3–4 weeks  | Every 3–4 weeks   | Every 3–4 weeks  |
| Ear cleaning          | Monthly           | Monthly          | Monthly           | Monthly          |
| Teeth brushing        | Daily (ideal) / 3x per week minimum | Same | Same | Same |
| Professional grooming | Every 8–12 weeks  | Every 6–8 weeks  | Rarely needed     | Every 6–8 weeks  |

**Notes:**
- Begin grooming routines early in a pet's life to build tolerance.
- Skipping dental care is the most common grooming oversight; dental disease affects 80% of pets by age 3.
- Check ears weekly for redness, odor, or discharge even if not cleaning.

## Hydration and Water Bowl Cleaning Frequency

| Task                        | Recommended Frequency          |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Refresh water in bowl       | At least twice daily           |
| Rinse bowl with hot water   | Daily                          |
| Full wash with soap/sanitize| Every 2–3 days (daily for cats prone to whisker fatigue — use wide, shallow bowls) |
| Replace plastic bowls       | Every 6 months (plastic harbors bacteria in scratches) |
| Clean water fountain filter | Weekly rinse; replace filter every 2–4 weeks |

**General hydration targets:**
- Dogs: approximately 1 oz of water per pound of body weight per day.
- Cats: approximately 3.5–4.5 oz per 5 lbs body weight per day (wet food contributes significantly).
- Rabbits: 1.5–5 oz per 2 lbs body weight per day.
- Birds: fresh water available at all times; change at minimum once daily.

**Warning signs of dehydration:**
- Dry or tacky gums
- Skin that does not spring back when gently pinched (skin tent test)
- Sunken eyes or lethargy
- Dark yellow urine or very dry droppings (birds/rabbits)
