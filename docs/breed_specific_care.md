# Breed-Specific Care Guidelines

## Dog Breeds — Exercise Needs

### 1. Labrador Retriever
**Energy Level:** High  
**Recommended Daily Exercise:** 60–80 minutes of vigorous activity

- Labradors are working/sporting dogs and require substantial daily exercise to prevent destructive behavior.
- Ideal activities: fetch, swimming, running, agility training, and off-leash play in a secure area.
- Without adequate exercise, Labs are prone to obesity, which exacerbates their genetic predisposition to hip and elbow dysplasia.
- **Special note:** Labradors are highly food-motivated and will eat beyond satiety — always use measured portions and factor in treats as part of daily caloric intake.
- **Special note:** Puppies under 18 months should avoid high-impact exercise (jumping, long runs) to protect developing joints; 5 minutes per month of age, twice daily is a safe guideline.

---

### 2. Poodle (Standard)
**Energy Level:** High  
**Recommended Daily Exercise:** 45–60 minutes

- Poodles are among the most intelligent dog breeds and need both physical and mental stimulation.
- Ideal activities: brisk walks, fetch, swimming (natural swimmers), obedience training, puzzle games.
- Miniature and Toy Poodles need 30–40 minutes daily; they tire more quickly due to smaller frames.
- **Special note:** Poodles are prone to boredom-driven anxiety; mental enrichment (training sessions, interactive toys) is as important as physical exercise.
- **Special note:** Their non-shedding curly coat requires professional grooming every 6–8 weeks to prevent matting; daily brushing is necessary between appointments.

---

### 3. Bulldog (English)
**Energy Level:** Low  
**Recommended Daily Exercise:** 20–30 minutes (split into two short sessions)

- Bulldogs are brachycephalic (flat-faced) and are extremely susceptible to overheating and respiratory distress.
- Exercise must be kept low-intensity; avoid any vigorous running or play in hot or humid conditions.
- Ideal activities: leisurely leash walks, gentle indoor play, slow-paced exploration.
- **Special note:** Never exercise a Bulldog in temperatures above 75°F (24°C) without close monitoring; they cannot pant efficiently to cool down.
- **Special note:** Bulldogs are prone to joint and spinal issues — avoid stairs where possible and never encourage jumping on or off furniture.

---

### 4. Siberian Husky
**Energy Level:** Very High  
**Recommended Daily Exercise:** 90–120 minutes of vigorous activity

- Huskies are bred as sled dogs and have extraordinary endurance; they require intensive daily exercise.
- Ideal activities: running, hiking, bikejoring, canicross, off-leash play in a fully fenced area (escape artists).
- An under-exercised Husky will howl, escape, chew, and destroy property — this is a breed that demands commitment.
- **Special note:** Huskies have a very strong prey drive and should never be off-leash in unfenced areas; recall training is difficult to make reliable.
- **Special note:** Despite their thick double coat, Huskies can adapt to warm climates, but exercise must be limited to early morning or late evening in summer and should never occur during the hottest parts of the day.

---

### 5. Chihuahua
**Energy Level:** Moderate (bursts of high energy)  
**Recommended Daily Exercise:** 20–30 minutes

- Chihuahuas are small but surprisingly active; they enjoy short play sessions and moderate walks.
- Ideal activities: indoor play, short leash walks, interactive toys, socialization sessions.
- Despite their small size, they should not be carried everywhere — they need to walk to maintain healthy weight and muscle tone.
- **Special note:** Chihuahuas are sensitive to cold due to their low body fat; a dog coat is recommended for outdoor activity below 50°F (10°C).
- **Special note:** Chihuahuas are prone to hypoglycemia (low blood sugar), especially as puppies; do not skip meals and monitor for trembling or weakness.

## Cat Breeds — Grooming Needs

### 1. Persian
**Coat Type:** Long, thick, silky double coat  
**Grooming Intensity:** Very High

- Daily brushing is mandatory to prevent severe matting — a missed day in humid conditions can result in painful mats requiring professional dematting or shaving.
- Use a wide-tooth metal comb followed by a slicker brush; start from the ends and work toward the roots.
- Professional grooming every 4–6 weeks is strongly recommended; many Persian owners opt for a "lion cut" in summer to reduce maintenance.
- **Special note:** Persian cats have flat faces (brachycephalic) and often have eye discharge; the facial folds and corner of the eyes must be wiped daily with a damp cotton pad to prevent staining and skin infections.
- **Special note:** Due to their sedentary nature, Persians are prone to obesity and dental disease; daily teeth brushing or dental treats should be incorporated into routine care.

---

### 2. Siamese
**Coat Type:** Short, fine, close-lying coat  
**Grooming Intensity:** Low

- Siamese cats are largely self-grooming; weekly brushing with a soft bristle brush or grooming glove is sufficient to remove loose hair and distribute natural oils.
- Bathing is rarely needed — typically only before a show or if the cat gets into something.
- Nail trimming every 3–4 weeks; dental brushing 3 times per week minimum.
- **Special note:** Siamese are extremely vocal and social; they can develop anxiety-related over-grooming (psychogenic alopecia) if left alone for extended periods — this is behavioral, not a skin condition.
- **Special note:** Siamese cats are prone to respiratory and dental issues; annual dental cleanings at the vet are recommended beginning at age 3.

---

### 3. Maine Coon
**Coat Type:** Long, dense, water-resistant coat with prominent ruff and tufted ears  
**Grooming Intensity:** Moderate to High

- Despite their long coat, Maine Coons are relatively easy to maintain compared to Persians; their fur is less prone to matting due to its silky texture.
- Brush 2–3 times per week using a stainless steel comb; daily brushing during spring and fall shedding seasons.
- Pay particular attention to the areas behind the ears, under the "armpits," and at the base of the tail where mats are most common.
- **Special note:** Maine Coons are the largest domestic cat breed and have large, tufted paws — check between the toes regularly for debris, litter buildup, or mats of fur that can cause discomfort or gait issues.
- **Special note:** Maine Coons are predisposed to hypertrophic cardiomyopathy (HCM) — a genetic heart condition. Annual cardiac screening with a veterinary cardiologist is recommended from age 2 onward, and any sudden change in breathing or exercise tolerance should be treated as an emergency.
